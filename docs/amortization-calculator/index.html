Amortization Schedule Calculator

Month-by-month loan schedule with extra payments and CSV export.

An amortization schedule shows exactly how a loan is paid off, one payment at a time. For every month it lists the payment date, how much goes to interest, how much reduces the balance, and what you still owe. Use this calculator for mortgages, car loans, personal loans and student loans with a fixed rate, and see the total cost of the loan before you sign.

How to use the amortization calculator

  1. Enter the loan amount, the annual interest rate and the term in years.
  2. Choose the month of your first payment so the schedule shows real dates.
  3. Optionally add an extra monthly principal payment to see how much sooner you’d finish.
  4. Open any year to see its monthly rows, or download the full schedule as a CSV file for Excel or Google Sheets.

What “amortization” means

An amortizing loan is repaid in equal monthly payments that cover both interest and principal. Each month, interest is charged on the balance you still owe. Whatever is left of the payment after interest reduces that balance. Because the balance is largest at the start, early payments are mostly interest; as the balance falls, more of each payment goes to principal. On a 30-year mortgage at 6.5%, it takes many years before principal makes up the larger share of the payment.

The formula

Payment = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)
Interest this month = balance × r
Principal this month = payment − interest

P is the loan amount, r the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n the number of payments. A $250,000 loan at 6.5% over 30 years has a monthly payment of about $1,580.17.

Total loan cost

The total cost of a loan is every payment added together: the amount you borrowed plus all the interest. The calculator shows this under “Total of payments”. For the example above, you would repay roughly $568,860, of which about $318,860 is interest. Comparing total cost, not just the monthly payment, is the clearest way to compare loan offers with different rates and terms.

How extra payments change the schedule

Extra money paid toward principal reduces the balance immediately, so every later month charges less interest. Even modest amounts matter: adding $200 a month to the example loan pays it off years early and saves tens of thousands of dollars in interest. Check with your lender that extra payments are applied to principal and that there is no prepayment penalty.

Frequently asked questions

Does the schedule include taxes and insurance?

No. It covers principal and interest only. For a house payment including property tax, insurance and PMI, use the mortgage calculator.

Why is my lender’s schedule slightly different?

Lenders round each payment to the cent and some calculate interest by days in the month. Differences are usually a few cents per month.

Can I use it for car loans?

Yes. Enter the amount financed, the APR and the term, for example 5 years for a 60-month loan.
