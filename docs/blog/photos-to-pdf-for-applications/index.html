How to Turn Photos Into a PDF for Applications and Forms

Published 2026-10-24

College admissions, job applications, rental applications, visa forms and insurance claims often ask you to upload documents as a PDF. If all you have is a phone, you can still produce a clean, professional-looking PDF in a few minutes. The trick is to take good photos first and then combine them correctly.

Step 1: take photos that are easy to read

  • Use daylight or bright, even light. Avoid the camera flash, which causes glare on glossy paper.
  • Place the document on a dark, plain surface. The contrast makes edges clear and looks better when cropped.
  • Hold the phone directly above the page, parallel to it, so the page is a rectangle rather than a trapezoid.
  • Fill the frame with the page, leaving a small border, and make sure all four corners are visible.
  • Check focus by zooming in on small print before moving to the next page.

Many phones also include a document scanner that straightens and crops pages automatically. On an iPhone it is in the Notes app (create a note, tap the camera icon, then “Scan Documents”); on Android, the Google Drive app has a Scan button. If you use these, they can save directly as a PDF.

Step 2: combine the photos into one PDF

Use JPG to PDF in your phone or computer browser:

  1. Choose all the photos. JPG, PNG and WebP all work.
  2. Put them in the right order with the arrow buttons.
  3. Choose US Letter for US forms (or A4 for most other countries) so the PDF prints at a normal paper size.
  4. Leave orientation on Automatic, so tall photos get portrait pages and wide photos get landscape pages.
  5. Click “Create PDF” and download.

The tool respects the rotation your phone recorded, so pages appear the right way up, and it adds no watermark. Your images are processed on your device, not uploaded.

Step 3: check the file size

Upload portals often set limits of a few megabytes per file. Phone photos are large, typically several megabytes each, so a five-page PDF can easily exceed a limit. If yours is too big:

  1. Run the photos through the Image Compressor first. Set the maximum width to about 1600 pixels and quality to around 75%. Text remains readable and each photo usually drops to a few hundred kilobytes.
  2. Create the PDF again from the compressed images.

As a rule of thumb, aim for around 150–300 KB per page for documents that will only be viewed on screen.

Step 4: name and review the file

  • Open the PDF and scroll through every page before you upload it. Check that nothing is cut off and that the order is right.
  • Name it clearly, for example Garcia-Maria-Proof-of-Address.pdf. Reviewers handle hundreds of files named scan.pdf.
  • If the form wants a single file containing several documents, create a PDF for each and then combine them with Merge PDF.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Uploading a photo of a screen. If you have the original digital document, save it as a PDF directly instead.
  • Cropping off edges. Some reviewers reject documents where page numbers, dates or signatures are cut off.
  • Mixing up front and back. For IDs and cards, put the front first, then the back, and say so in the form if there is a notes field.
  • Sharing more than needed. Cover or remove information that was not requested, such as full account numbers, if the instructions allow it.

Doing it on a computer

The same steps work on Windows and Mac: transfer the photos from your phone (by cable, AirDrop, or a cloud drive), then use JPG to PDF in your browser. A larger screen makes it easier to spot blurry pages before you submit.
