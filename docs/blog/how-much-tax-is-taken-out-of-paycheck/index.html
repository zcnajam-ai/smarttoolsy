How Much Tax Is Taken Out of a Paycheck in 2026?

Published 2026-10-29

If you have ever compared your salary with the amount that lands in your bank account, you know the gap can be large. For most workers, between about 15% and 30% of each paycheck goes to taxes. Exactly how much depends on your pay, your filing status and where you live. This guide explains each deduction using the 2026 figures, and the paycheck calculator does the math for you.

What comes out of a paycheck

  • Federal income tax, based on your W-4 and the IRS brackets.
  • Social Security tax: 6.2% of wages.
  • Medicare tax: 1.45% of wages, plus 0.9% on wages over $200,000.
  • State and local income tax, in most states.
  • Deductions you chose, such as a 401(k), health insurance or a flexible spending account.

Social Security and Medicare together are called FICA taxes. Your employer pays a matching 7.65% on top of your pay, which you never see on your stub.

How brackets really work

A common worry is that a raise could push you into a higher bracket and leave you with less money. That can’t happen with income tax, because each rate applies only to the slice of income inside its bracket. If you are single with $60,000 of taxable income, you pay 10% on the first $12,400, 12% on the next $38,000 and 22% only on the last $9,600. Your “bracket” is 22%, but your overall federal rate is much lower.

Ways to keep more of each paycheck

  • Pre-tax retirement savings. Money you put in a traditional 401(k) or 403(b) is not counted for federal income tax this year. Saving $200 per paycheck cuts your take-home pay by only about $156 to $176, depending on whether you are in the 22% or 12% bracket.
  • Pre-tax benefits. Health insurance premiums, HSA and FSA contributions taken through your employer usually avoid income tax and FICA.
  • Update your W-4. If you got a large refund last year, too much was withheld. If you owed money, add extra withholding. The IRS Tax Withholding Estimator can help.
  • Claim credits for children. The child tax credit is $2,200 per qualifying child in 2026. Listing children in Step 3 of the W-4 lowers withholding through the year.

New deductions for tips and overtime

For tax years 2025 through 2028, workers can deduct up to $25,000 of qualified tips, and up to $12,500 of qualified overtime pay ($25,000 for a married couple filing jointly). The overtime deduction covers only the extra “half” in time-and-a-half pay. Both start to phase out above $150,000 of income ($300,000 for joint filers). They reduce federal income tax only: Social Security and Medicare are still taken out of tips and overtime as usual. Because they are claimed on your tax return, many workers will see the benefit as a larger refund rather than in each check.

Frequently asked questions

What percentage of my paycheck goes to taxes?

For a single person earning $60,000 in a state without income tax, about 16% goes to federal tax and FICA. At $100,000 it is about 21%. State taxes add more in most states.

Why is my first paycheck of the year different?

Benefit elections often change in January, and the Social Security wage limit resets. High earners notice this most: once their wages pass $184,500 in 2026, Social Security stops being withheld for the rest of the year.

Is overtime taxed more?

No. Overtime is taxed at the same rates as other pay, but a large paycheck can make withholding look higher because the payroll system treats it as if you earned that much all year. It evens out when you file your return.
