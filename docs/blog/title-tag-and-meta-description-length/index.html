Meta Title Length and Meta Description Length: 2026 Guide

Published 2026-10-25

How long should a meta title be? Write a title that’s too long and Google cuts it off with “…”, often right before the words that matter. Write one that’s too short and you waste space that could persuade someone to click. Here are the lengths that work, and why the real limit is measured in pixels rather than characters.

The short answer

Aim forGoogle shows up to about
Title tag50–60 characters600 pixels on desktop
Meta description (desktop)120–160 characters920 pixels
Meta description (mobile)Key message in the first 120 charactersAbout 680 pixels

These are guides, not hard rules. Google doesn’t publish official limits and adjusts its layout from time to time. Test your own text in the SERP preview tool, which measures it in pixels.

Why pixels matter more than characters

Search results have a fixed width, so Google truncates text when it runs out of room. Letters have different widths: a capital W takes up several times the space of a lowercase i. A 55-character title in narrow letters fits easily; a 50-character title full of capitals and wide letters may not.

Compare:

  • “Illinois little-league fields list”: narrow letters, fits comfortably.
  • “WOW! MEGA HOME MAKEOVER WORKBOOK”: fewer characters, but capitals make it far wider.

Avoid writing titles in all capitals. Besides looking shouty, they get cut off much sooner.

How to write a title tag that fits

  1. Start with the topic. Put the main keyword in the first few words, where it’s never truncated.
  2. Add the benefit or detail that earns the click: “free”, a year, a number, or the specific result.
  3. Put your brand at the end, if at all. “ – SmartToolsy” is the first thing to be cut, which is fine.
  4. Use separators sparingly. One dash or pipe is enough.
  5. Make every title unique, so Google can tell your pages apart.

Example: “Roofing Calculator – Roof Area, Squares & Shingle Bundles” puts the keyword first and then answers what the tool gives you.

How to write a meta description that fits

  • Say what the page gives the reader, in one or two plain sentences.
  • Include the main keyword naturally; Google bolds words that match the search.
  • Put the most important point in the first 120 characters so it survives on mobile.
  • End with a reason to act, such as “Free, no sign-up” or “With worked examples”.

Why Google sometimes ignores your title and description

Google rewrites titles when they’re too long, stuffed with keywords, the same across many pages, or don’t match what the page is about. It often uses the page’s main heading (H1) instead, so keeping your title and H1 closely aligned reduces rewrites. Descriptions are replaced with page text even more often, whenever another passage matches the search better. You can’t prevent this completely, but clear, accurate tags are used far more often than vague or spammy ones.

Common questions

  • Is a longer title bad for SEO? Not in itself. Google still reads the whole title; it just can’t display all of it. The risk is losing important words or getting your title rewritten.
  • How short is too short? Titles under about 30 characters often leave out useful detail. Use the space to describe the page fully.
  • Do the limits differ on mobile? Mobile titles can wrap onto two lines, so they’re slightly more forgiving, but descriptions are shorter. Writing for desktop and front-loading the key words covers both.
