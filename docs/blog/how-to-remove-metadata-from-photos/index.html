How to Remove Metadata from Photos (iPhone, Android, Windows, Mac)

Published 2026-10-25

Open the details of almost any photo taken on a phone and you’ll find more than you expected: the exact GPS coordinates where it was taken, the date and time to the second, the phone model, and sometimes the owner’s name. This hidden information is called metadata, and it travels with the file whenever you email it, send it as a document, or upload it somewhere that doesn’t strip it.

Here’s how to see what your photos reveal and remove it on every common device.

The quickest way on any device

  1. Open the Image Metadata Remover in your browser.
  2. Choose one or more photos. The report shows what each one contains, with GPS location and serial numbers highlighted.
  3. Download the clean copies.

The photos are processed on your own device and never uploaded. Only the hidden data is removed, so the picture keeps its full quality, and portrait photos still display the right way up.

On iPhone

  • Remove location when sharing: in Photos, select the picture, tap Share, tap Options at the top, and turn off Location. This applies to that share only.
  • Remove location from the photo itself: open the photo, swipe up to see its details, tap Adjust next to the location, and choose No Location.
  • Stop recording location: go to Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Camera and choose Never.

These options handle location, but not the camera model, date or other fields. For a fully clean file, use the browser tool above in Safari. iPhones save photos as HEIC by default; set Settings → Camera → Formats → Most Compatible to save JPGs instead, which every tool can clean.

On Android

  • Stop recording location: open your Camera app’s settings and turn off the option usually called Location tags or Save location. The name varies by phone maker.
  • In Google Photos: look in Settings → Sharing for an option to remove geo location from items you share by link.
  • For a fully clean copy: open the metadata remover in Chrome and pick the photos from your gallery.

On Windows

  1. Right-click the photo in File Explorer and choose Properties.
  2. Open the Details tab and click Remove Properties and Personal Information at the bottom.
  3. Choose Create a copy with all possible properties removed, or pick individual fields to remove.

This works well for JPG files. It can’t touch every type of metadata, such as some XMP blocks added by editing apps, so check the result if privacy really matters.

On a Mac

  • Preview: open the photo, choose Tools → Show Inspector, click the (i) tab, open GPS, and click Remove Location Info. This removes location only.
  • Photos app: when exporting (File → Export), untick the option to include location information.

What photo metadata can reveal

GPS locationCan pinpoint your home, workplace or children’s school to within a few meters.

Don’t social networks remove it anyway?

Many large platforms strip location and most EXIF data when you upload a photo, but you can’t count on it everywhere. Email attachments, files shared as documents in messaging apps, cloud-storage links, forum uploads and marketplace listings frequently keep the original file, metadata included. Cleaning photos yourself before sharing is the only way to be sure.

Common questions

  • Does removing metadata change the photo? Not with a lossless tool. The metadata remover copies the image data unchanged. Screenshots and re-saving in an editor also drop metadata, but they re-compress the image.
  • Should businesses remove metadata from website images? It’s good practice. It removes staff names and locations, and slightly reduces file size. For bigger savings, also run images through the image compressor.
  • Can metadata be recovered after removal? Not from the clean copy. It’s gone from that file, though your original still has it.
